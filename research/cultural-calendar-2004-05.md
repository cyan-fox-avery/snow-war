# Snow War — Cultural Calendar: Winter 2004–05

**Purpose:** A week-by-week cultural timeline (Sept 2004 → Mar 2005) so the game can run timed cultural events alongside its weather season. Each entry is written as a game-usable event line in a kid's-eye view.

**Method note:** Weekly #1s come from two verified sources: the Billboard-based Canadian singles chart (physical sales — a weak proxy for airplay, used with caution) and the Mediabase Canadian rock airplay chart (a much better proxy for what was actually on the radio). Broader "what was hot" texture comes from top40-charts.com's weekly Canada Top 20 snapshots. Where sources are silent, weeks are marked as gaps — nothing is invented.

**Anchor rule (Avery's):** entries reflect what was *popular during* that week, not release dates.

---

## September 2004 — back to school

**Week of Sept 6**
- 🎵 Fantasia's "I Believe" (American Idol winner) sits at #1 on the sales chart; Usher's "Yeah!" is still everywhere at #2 — the song of the year refuses to leave.
- 🃏 The first Yu-Gi-Oh! banlist dropped in August, and playground duels just got serious — banned cards are playground legend now.

**Week of Sept 13**
- 🏒 **September 16: the NHL lockout begins.** Commissioner Bettman announces the season won't start. On every TV in Canada, grown-ups use words kids aren't supposed to repeat.
- 🎵 "I Believe" still #1; the charts are holding their breath.

**Week of Sept 20**
- 🎵 **"American Idiot" hits #1 in Canada** — and the album drops September 21. The punk event of the fall; every kid with an older sibling just discovered Green Day, and the secret's spreading at recess.
- 🎸 On rock radio, The Killers' "Somebody Told Me" is still all over the airwaves.

**Week of Sept 27**
- 🎵 "American Idiot" #1 again — the sales chart belongs to Green Day while radio catches up.

---

## October 2004 — the war begins

**Week of Oct 4**
- 🎸 Velvet Revolver's "Fall to Pieces" takes #1 on rock radio — the older kids' anthem this week.
- 🎵 "American Idiot" still #1 on the sales chart.

**Week of Oct 11**
- 🎸 **U2's "Vertigo" seizes #1 on rock radio** — and it's going to stay there a long, long time. That "uno, dos, tres, catorce!" countdown is about to be hummed in every hallway.
- 🎬 Hilary Duff's *Raise Your Voice* opens (Oct 8) — the Duff sisters own the elementary demographic outright this month.

**Week of Oct 18**
- 🎵 **"Yeah!" returns to #1 in Canada** (Oct 16) — Usher's victory lap; the song of the year gets a second coronation.
- 📺 **October 16: *Movie Night in Canada* premieres.** With hockey gone, CBC puts Ron MacLean in empty junior rinks to introduce Saturday-night movies — *Dinosaur*, *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, *Jaws*. Saturday nights feel wrong in a way kids can sense even if they can't name.
- 📊 New on the charts: Canadian Idol winner Kalan Porter's "Awake in a Dream" debuts at #1 on the Top 20 — the whole country just voted for this kid.

**Week of Oct 25**
- 🎵 **"Awake in a Dream" takes #1 on the sales chart** — Kalan Porter's 8-week reign begins. Canadian Idol fever is at full strength; everyone knows someone who voted 50 times.

---

## November 2004 — arms race

**Week of Nov 1**
- 🎵 "Awake in a Dream" #1, week two.
- 🎬 **November 5: *The Incredibles* opens.** The movie event of the fall — every kid leaves the theatre arguing about which power they'd pick, and fort designs suddenly include "force fields."

**Week of Nov 8**
- 🎵 "Awake in a Dream" #1, week three.
- 🎸 "Vertigo" still #1 on rock radio — five weeks and counting.
- 🎬 November 10: *The Polar Express* — the Christmas-movie season officially starts.

**Week of Nov 15**
- 🎵 "Awake in a Dream" #1, week four.
- 🎬 **November 19: the great Friday-night dilemma — *The SpongeBob SquarePants Movie* AND *National Treasure* open the same day.** Playground alliances fracture along movie lines.

**Week of Nov 22**
- 🎵 "Awake in a Dream" #1, week five.
- 🎮 **November 21: the Nintendo DS launches in North America.** The Christmas grail just appeared in stores — every kid now knows exactly what to put at the top of the list, and exactly who in class will get one.
- 🎬 November 24: *Christmas with the Kranks* — the adults' Christmas movie; kids endure it for the popcorn.

**Week of Nov 29**
- 🎵 **November 29: Green Day releases "Boulevard of Broken Dreams" as a single.** The winter anthem arrives mid-season — within weeks it'll be the song of every grey afternoon, hummed on the walk home in the dark.
- 🎵 "Awake in a Dream" #1, week six — Kalan Porter's reign rolls on.

---

## December 2004 — deep freeze

**Week of Dec 6**
- 🎵 "Awake in a Dream" #1, week seven — the Idol winner owns December.
- 🎸 "Vertigo" *still* #1 on rock radio — two months straight.

**Week of Dec 13**
- 🎸 **"Boulevard of Broken Dreams" seizes #1 on rock radio** (Dec 17) — the changing of the guard; U2's long reign ends and Green Day's winter begins.
- 🎬 December 17: *Lemony Snicket's A Series of Unfortunate Events* — Jim Carrey is huge, and the books were already playground currency.

**Week of Dec 20**
- 🎵 **"Do They Know It's Christmas?" — Band Aid 20 — takes #1** (Dec 18). The charity single owns Christmas week, just like the original did twenty years earlier.
- 🎄 Christmas morning: the DS grail lands under trees across the country — one kid per class becomes royalty for the holidays.

**Week of Dec 27**
- 🎵 Band Aid 20 #1, week two.
- ❄️ Christmas break — the war pauses. Forts sit empty under fresh snow; everyone returns to find the battlefield changed.

---

## January 2005 — the grind

**Week of Jan 3**
- 🎵 Back to school. Band Aid 20 #1 (through Jan 15) — the Christmas song overstays slightly, as Christmas songs do.
- 🎸 "Boulevard of Broken Dreams" #1 on rock radio — the official sound of January: grey skies, grey snowbanks.

**Week of Jan 10**
- 🎸 "Boulevard" #1 rock, week four. *Gap: weekly pop-airplay detail unverified for this stretch — the sales chart is quiet between Christmas and the next #1.*

**Week of Jan 17**
- 🎸 "Boulevard" #1 rock, week five.

**Week of Jan 24**
- 🎵 **"My Boo" — Usher & Alicia Keys — takes #1** (Jan 22). The slow jam of the winter; school-dance season is coming and everyone knows it.
- 🎸 "Boulevard" #1 rock, week six.

**Week of Jan 31**
- 🎵 "My Boo" #1, week two.

---

## February 2005 — false spring

**Week of Feb 7**
- 🎵 "My Boo" #1, week three.
- 🎸 "Boulevard" #1 rock, week eight — its grip is loosening.

**Week of Feb 14**
- 💌 Valentine's Day: the classroom card exchange — everyone gets one, the good ones are handmade, the diplomats of the playground are made here.
- 🎵 "My Boo" #1, week four.
- 🏒 **February 16: the season is officially cancelled.** Bettman kills the 2004–05 NHL season — first time since 1919 no Stanley Cup is awarded. The lockout's death certificate arrives mid-winter; the Cup will read "2004–05 Season Not Played."

**Week of Feb 21**
- 🎵 **"All Because of You" — U2 — takes #1** (Feb 26), on sales and rock radio simultaneously. U2's second coronation of the winter.
- 🎸 Rock chart: "All Because of You" #1 (Feb 18) — "Boulevard"'s nine-week reign ends.

**Week of Feb 28**
- 🎵 "All Because of You" #1, week two.

---

## March 2005 — the melt

**Week of Mar 7**
- 🎵 "All Because of You" #1 (through Mar 12).
- 🌤️ The thaw begins — forts slump, the creek bed runs, everything gets urgent.

**Week of Mar 14**
- 🎵 *Gap: no verified #1 transition detail for this specific week — the chart moves from U2's run toward the next #1.*
- 🌧️ Slush season. The Big Pile shrinks daily; the war's geography is literally melting.

**Week of Mar 21**
- 🎵 **"Paper Rain" — Amanda Stott — takes #1** (Mar 19). A Canadian Idol-adjacent voice closes out the winter's chart story.
- 🌱 Dandelions soon. Someone asks: "What do we play next?"

**Week of Mar 28**
- 🎵 "Paper Rain" #1, week two.
- 🌼 The melt completes. The forts are gone, the boundaries vanish, the snowbanks turn grey. Winter 2004–05 is over — remembered.

## Source gaps (honest accounting)

1. **Weekly pop airplay (the biggest gap):** The pre-2007 Billboard Canada chart was physical-sales only — a weak proxy for what kids actually heard. The Mediabase rock airplay chart covers rock only. No verified source was found for week-by-week pop/Top-40 Canadian airplay (CHUM FM, etc.) or MuchMusic's Top 30 countdowns for this period. Reigning #1s are used as the verified backbone; finer airplay texture is inferred, not sourced.
2. **top40-charts.com weekly snapshots:** Only four weekly Canada Top 20 snapshots could be retrieved (Jul 24, Aug 14, Sep 4, Oct 16, 2004). Further weekly fetches were blocked (HTTP 403), so Nov 2004 → Mar 2005 has no snapshot-level detail — only the #1 runs.
3. **Mid-January 2005 and mid-March 2005:** No verified chart-transition detail for these specific weeks; marked as gaps above.
4. **Québec francophone radio:** Not researched at this granularity (Mira's standing flag). CKOI and French-language charts for winter 2004–05 remain an open research item — needed so the Québec side isn't just translated anglophone references.
5. **Trend dates at week precision:** Some beats are month-level (e.g., exactly when the DS sold out regionally; when "Boulevard" peaked on pop vs. rock radio). Placed at their best-verified week; treat as approximate.
6. **Movie release dates** verified via Box Office Mojo / Movie Insider. **Movie Night in Canada** premiere (Oct 16, 2004) and lockout dates verified via Wikipedia. **NHL lockout timeline** (Sept 16, 2004 → Feb 16, 2005) verified via multiple sources.
